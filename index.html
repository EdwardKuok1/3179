Rental Affordability in Victoria (2025)

Exploring how rent prices vary across regions of Victoria

Victoria Median Rent (2025)

The median weekly rent in rural Victoria is on average $100 lower than the Greater Melbourne area, with most regions falling between $200–$350 per week. Loddon – Elmore records the lowest rent at $190 per week.

Noticeably, the east side of rural Victoria has a higher median rent than the west side, which may be influenced by its proximity to the Australian Capital Territory and New South Wales, as well as stronger economic links with regional hubs like Albury–Wodonga and East Gippsland.

These lower rental prices reflect the reduced housing demand and cost of living in less densely populated areas, though it may also indicate fewer employment opportunities and slower population growth.

Greater Melbourne Median Rent (2025)

The Greater Melbourne area has a higher median rent than rural Victoria, ranging from $300–$550 per week. Inner Melbourne averages around $500 per week, with Melbourne City itself being the highest at $550 per week.

Higher rents in the city core reflect the concentration of employment, universities, and services, driving strong housing demand. Outer suburban areas such as Whittlesea and Macedon Ranges remain more affordable, providing lower-cost options for renters willing to commute.

The sharp contrast between central and outer Melbourne highlights the ongoing challenges of housing affordability and urban sprawl in Victoria’s most populated region.

Australia Median Rent (2025)

Nationally, rents peak in the larger capital cities, led by Greater Sydney at $796 per week and Greater Perth at $721. More affordable regions remain outside the capitals, with Rest of South Australia at $446 and Rest of Tasmania at $469. In most states, capital city areas outprice their regional counterparts; Queensland is relatively close, with Greater Brisbane at $687 and Rest of Qld at $668.

Capital vs Regional Rents by State (2025)

Capitals generally outprice their regional counterparts, with the largest gaps in New South Wales and Western Australia. The Australian Capital Territory appears only once as it represents both capital and territory. Queensland shows a relatively small difference between Greater Brisbane and the rest of the state.

Top 10 Highest Median Rents in Victoria (2025)

The highest median rents cluster around inner metropolitan Melbourne and coastal lifestyle areas such as the Surf Coast. These markets reflect stronger demand pressures from employment, amenities, and sea-change preferences.

Distribution of Victoria Median Rents (2025)

Most Victoria regions fall between $300 and $400 per week, with a long right tail driven by high-demand inner-city localities and affluent coastal pockets.
